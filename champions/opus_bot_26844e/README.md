# opus_bot

## Strategy

An exploitative equity/EV bot. It learns from the match history how often each opponent folds to bets of each size and how often they open, bet and raise. It narrows each opponent's range from what they did this hand, runs an eval7 Monte Carlo of its equity against those ranges, and picks fold, check/call or one of a handful of bet sizes using a one-street EV model.

- **Preflop:** equity against each opponent's narrowed preflop range. Ranges come from `data/preflop.json`, which holds every one of the 169 hand classes ranked by heads-up equity against a random hand (generated by `tools/gen_preflop.py`). Opponents who haven't acted yet count only with their estimated continue probability. Open sizes are 2 / 2.5 / 3 / 4 bb (plus 1 bb per limper); against a raise it considers 3x, pot-relative re-raises and a jam. Most hands are opened when the learned fold rates make steals profitable.
- **Postflop:** equity against the opponent's range after a further cut: their preflop range is ranked by made-hand strength on the board, and the top fraction implied by their bets, raises and calls this hand is kept.
- **Bet sizing:** the candidates are 33/50/75/100/150% pot and a jam, or pot-relative raises and a min-raise. EV(bet) = P(all fold)·pot + P(call)·(equity vs the calling range · final pot − cost). The calling range is the top (1 − fold rate) of the opponent's range. A raise must beat the best passive option by 2% of the pot.
- **Opponent modelling:** per-opponent fold rates come from a Beta prior in 5 buckets of `owed / pot` (edges 0.26 / 0.34 / 0.45 / 0.55), split by preflop/postflop. Separate tables cover an opponent re-raising after it already raised this street ("_rr"), whose range is much stronger. Fold equity is scaled down for tiny bets (< 20% of the pot after the bet) and for ranges that already called down (× √postflop range fraction). Raise frequencies narrow ranges, softened for frequent aggressors (a 70% raiser is treated as nearly random). The model is rebuilt from `match_action_log` by replaying each hand's betting to recover streets and pot sizes.
- **Time budget:** 1500 Monte Carlo trials heads-up, or 900 multiway plus 300 per raise size. Typical decisions take ~5 ms; the max seen is ~19 ms, far inside the 2 s limit.

## Known weaknesses

- The EV model looks one street ahead: it ignores implied odds and the risk of being re-raised (beyond tightening the calling range).
- Opponents' made-hand ranking ignores draws, so ranges that bet draws look stronger than they are.
- Hand outcomes are invisible in cash mode (no showdown cards, stacks reset), so it can't tell "raises 70% at random" from "raises the top 70%". It assumes frequent aggressors are partly random.
- Against a strong, balanced opponent that rarely folds preflop, the wide steals and EV-maximising jams make for high variance.
